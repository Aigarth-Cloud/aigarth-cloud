## 1. Two kinds of governance

Aigarth Cloud operates with two distinct governance bodies:

1. **Company governance** — the legal entity that employs the team, owns the brand, and runs the business
2. **Protocol governance** — on-chain decisions about the Aigarth protocol (stake, burn rate, treasury, parameter changes)

This document covers **company governance**. Protocol governance is defined in [`ROADMAP.md`](../../../ROADMAP.md) Phase 14 and is governed by QUBIC stake weight.

## 2. Company structure

- **Legal entity:** A Delaware C-corp (or equivalent in founding jurisdiction)
- **Founders:** Equal equity split by default, with vesting (4 years, 1-year cliff)
- **Advisors:** Equity or tokens, defined per advisor agreement
- **Employees:** Salary + equity grants, defined per offer letter
- **Service providers:** Independent contractor or vendor agreement

## 3. Decision-making

### 3.1 Routine decisions (daily)

- Made by the responsible person listed in `TEAM-AND-ROLES.md`
- No formal sign-off required
- Documented when it affects a doc in `now/docs/`

### 3.2 Operational decisions (weekly)

- Made by the relevant lead (e.g. Head of Engineering on architecture choices)
- Reviewed in the weekly team sync
- Logged in the relevant doc

### 3.3 Strategic decisions (monthly or as needed)

- Made by the CEO, in consultation with relevant leads
- Requires a written decision memo (1 page max) covering: context, options, decision, rationale, downstream impact
- Logged in the relevant doc with date

### 3.4 Constitutional decisions (rare)

- Equity grants, founder changes, fundraising terms, major pivots
- Requires unanimous founder consent
- Legal review before signing anything
- Logged in a board minutes document

## 5. Communication norms

- **Disagreements:** Disagree and commit. If you disagree with a decision, say so, get your view on the record, then execute on the decision.
- **Surprises:** No surprises. If something is going to miss, escalate early.
- **Bad news:** Travels up faster than good news. Send it up.
- **Credit:** Public. Everyone knows who did what.
- **Blame:** Private. Errors are learning opportunities, not public callouts.

## 6. Conflict resolution

1. Direct conversation between the two parties first
2. If unresolved, escalate to the relevant lead
3. If still unresolved, escalate to the CEO
4. Final decision rests with the CEO for company matters, CTO for technical matters, founders jointly for constitutional matters

## 7. Hiring and firing

- **Hiring:** Hiring manager proposes, CEO approves budget, CTO approves technical fit (for eng roles)
- **Performance issues:** Coaching first, then PIP, then transition
- **Terminations:** CEO + relevant lead + legal sign-off

## 8. Fundraising governance

- **Pre-seed / seed:** CEO drives, CTO supports. No board approval needed.
- **Series A and beyond:** Board approval required.
- **Token-related decisions:** Coordinated with legal and protocol governance.
